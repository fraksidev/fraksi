# Project vision

Fraksi explores how digital systems can preserve and extend the liveness,
responsiveness, shared agency, and expressive constraints of puppetry and
related performance traditions. The immediate medium is virtual shadow and
cut-out puppetry; the broader intent includes live control of characters,
props, scenery, cameras, lighting, sound, projection, effects, physical
mechanisms, and interactive installations.

The project favors continuous performance over canned action. Hardware should
be replaceable without redesigning a character: a performer station emits
normalized expressive signals, a separate patch layer routes them through
bounded histories and explicit processors to authored puppet, prop, or scenery
ports, constraints produce canonical state, and engine adapters render or act
on that state.

Puppet mechanics are authored rather than inferred from a universal human
skeleton. Visual realism is secondary to readable intention, rhythm,
relationship, resistance, and playability. AI may assist offline authoring, but
it is not required in the live path and should not remove the puppeteer's
judgment.

Early work should remain small, playable, diagnosable, and suitable for actual
performance studies. Premature goals include neither a complete animation
suite nor generalized whole-body optimization, photorealism, automatic acting,
or a universal node editor.

Later ensemble productions may add human director or stage-manager stations
that cue scenes and sets, operate scenery/cameras/lights/sound, and reassign
control patches. These remain authorized participants in the same live system;
they do not move production authority into a renderer or replace performer
agency by default.

## Project-owner input required

The repository does not establish the following choices:

- The acceptance performance budget and initial art direction for
  `demos/shared-duet`, which will support Canvas and Three.js over shared
  stage/medium/scene/cast foundations.
- The next evaluation milestone and participant scenario to optimize for.
- Whether either reference application should eventually migrate to the new
  shared foundation after the third demo proves it; no migration is currently
  planned.
- The desired public deployment, privacy, identity, recording, and retention
  policies beyond the current trusted local-network prototypes.
- The artistic direction, cultural context, commissioning/licensing plan, and
  attribution requirements for production puppet artwork.
- Priority among authoring tools, recording/replay, richer handoffs and
  constraints, additional input devices, OSC, Unreal, and broader stage output.

Until those decisions are made, `docs/roadmap.md` keeps candidate priorities
explicitly undecided.
